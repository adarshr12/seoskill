# Books Every Product Manager Should Read

The books below are widely and consistently recommended across the product management community, covering foundational strategy, discovery, prioritization, and growth. This isn't an exhaustive list, but a solid, well-regarded starting point for building broad, practical PM knowledge.

## Quick facts

- These books are widely cited across PM communities, blogs, and courses as foundational reading.
- Reading alone won't build PM skill — pairing these ideas with real application and practice matters more than the reading itself.
- See related: [How to Build Product Intuition Faster](/pm-career/how-to-build-product-intuition-faster), where studying others' frameworks and decisions (including through books) is one accelerant.

## Foundational product strategy and process

**"Inspired" by Marty Cagan** — A widely referenced overview of how strong product organizations operate, covering product discovery, team structure, and the difference between building what's requested versus building what genuinely creates value.

**"Escaping the Build Trap" by Melissa Perri** — Focuses specifically on the difference between output (features shipped) and outcome (real value delivered), a distinction central to effective prioritization and product strategy.

## Discovery and customer understanding

**"Continuous Discovery Habits" by Teresa Torres** — A practical, habit-based approach to ongoing customer discovery, covering how to build a sustainable practice of talking to users and testing assumptions continuously, rather than treating discovery as a one-time phase.

**"The Mom Test" by Rob Fitzpatrick** — A short, practical guide specifically on how to ask customers questions that produce genuinely useful, honest signal, rather than questions that lead to flattering but misleading answers.

## Growth and metrics

**"Hooked" by Nir Eyal** — Covers the psychology behind habit-forming products, relevant for PMs thinking about engagement and retention design, though worth reading with awareness of the ethical considerations around habit-forming product design.

**"Measure What Matters" by John Doerr** — Covers the OKR (Objectives and Key Results) goal-setting framework in depth, relevant for PMs working within or introducing OKR-based planning.

## Prioritization and market strategy

**"Crossing the Chasm" by Geoffrey Moore** — A classic on technology adoption lifecycle and market strategy, particularly relevant for PMs navigating the transition from early adopters to mainstream market adoption.

## Career and interview preparation

**"Cracking the PM Interview" by Gayle Laakmann McDowell and Jackie Bavaro** — A widely used resource specifically for PM interview preparation, covering common question types and structured approaches to answering them.

## How to get the most value from PM books

Reading alone builds conceptual knowledge, but real skill develops through applying these ideas to genuine work — after reading a chapter or framework, look for an opportunity to actually apply it to a real product problem you're facing, rather than treating the reading as complete once finished. Discussing these books with peers or a mentor, comparing how the ideas map (or don't) to your own specific context, often deepens the learning further than solitary reading alone.

## Common mistakes when building a PM reading habit

- **Reading passively without applying concepts to real work**, missing the deeper, more durable learning that comes from genuine application.
- **Trying to read everything at once**, rather than pacing reading alongside genuine reflection and application time.
- **Treating any single book as a complete framework** for all situations, rather than one useful perspective among several worth understanding.
- **Not discussing or debating ideas with peers**, missing the deeper understanding that comes from testing concepts against others' real experience and pushback.

## FAQ

**Should a new PM start with a specific book from this list first?**
"Inspired" is commonly recommended as a strong foundational starting point, given its broad coverage of core product management concepts, though the best starting point can depend on your specific current gaps or interests.

**Are these books relevant for BAs and project managers too?**
Many of the underlying concepts — discovery, prioritization, customer understanding — are genuinely useful across product, business analysis, and project management roles, even though the books are framed specifically around product management.

**How often should a PM read a new book to keep learning?**
There's no fixed pace — what matters more is genuinely absorbing and applying what you read, rather than optimizing for reading volume alone.

**Are podcasts a good alternative to books for ongoing PM learning?**
Yes — see [Podcasts Every Product Manager Should Follow](/pm-career/podcasts-every-product-manager-should-follow) for a complementary, often more time-efficient way to stay current with PM thinking and practice.
