# What Is an Epic Owner in SAFe?

An **Epic Owner** in SAFe is responsible for defining, driving, and coordinating a large, portfolio-level Epic through its lifecycle — from initial concept through the portfolio Kanban process, working with relevant stakeholders to refine the Epic until it's genuinely ready for ARTs or Solution Trains to begin delivering it.

## Quick facts

- Epic Owners operate at the portfolio level, distinct from Product Management (ART level) or Solution Management (Solution Train level).
- An Epic Owner typically manages an Epic through the portfolio Kanban's stages, from funnel through analysis to implementation.
- This connects to [What Is Lean Portfolio Management in SAFe](/safe/what-is-lean-portfolio-management-in-safe), the broader governance context Epic Owners operate within.

## Where the Epic Owner role sits in SAFe's structure

| Level | Role | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio | Epic Owner | Epics (large, portfolio-level initiatives) |
| Solution Train | Solution Management | Capabilities |
| ART | Product Management | Features |
| Team | Product Owner | Stories |

## What an Epic Owner actually does

An Epic Owner defines the Epic clearly — the business problem it addresses and the value it's expected to deliver — and shepherds it through SAFe's portfolio Kanban process, which typically includes stages like funnel (initial idea), analysis (further refinement and validation), and eventually implementation (once approved and ready for ARTs or Solution Trains to begin delivering). This involves coordinating with relevant stakeholders, building the business case, and ensuring the Epic is well-defined enough before it's handed off for actual delivery.

## Why the Epic Owner role exists

Large, portfolio-level initiatives often start as broad, somewhat undefined ideas that need genuine refinement and validation before they're ready for teams to actually build — without a dedicated role responsible for this refinement, large Epics can either stall indefinitely in vague form, or get pushed into delivery prematurely, before genuine understanding of the problem and value is established. The Epic Owner role exists specifically to drive this crucial upfront definition and validation work at the portfolio level.

## A worked example

A large retail company's leadership identifies a broad opportunity: "Enable a unified customer experience across online and in-store shopping." An Epic Owner is assigned to this Epic, responsible for further defining and validating it — conducting initial analysis on the business case, working with relevant stakeholders across the affected ARTs to understand feasibility, and refining the Epic's scope and expected value before it moves from the portfolio Kanban's analysis stage into implementation. Once sufficiently defined and approved, the Epic Owner works with the relevant Solution Train or ARTs to break it down into Capabilities and Features for actual delivery, though day-to-day delivery execution then shifts to Solution Management and Product Management at their respective levels.

## Common mistakes with the Epic Owner role

- **Rushing an Epic into implementation without sufficient upfront definition and validation**, risking teams building against a poorly understood, insufficiently refined initiative.
- **Confusing the Epic Owner role with Product Management or Solution Management**, when the Epic Owner specifically operates at the broader, portfolio level with a distinct focus on initial Epic definition.
- **Not maintaining the Epic Owner's involvement through the portfolio Kanban stages**, losing continuity and accountability for the Epic's development and refinement.
- **Assigning Epic Owner responsibility without sufficient authority or organizational support** to actually drive the cross-functional coordination the role requires.

## FAQ

**Is Epic Owner a full-time dedicated role?**
This varies by organization — some assign it as a dedicated responsibility for significant, ongoing Epics; others treat it as an additional responsibility for someone (often a senior product or business leader) already holding another role.

**How is an Epic Owner different from Product Management?**
Epic Owners operate at the broader portfolio level, focused on defining and validating large Epics before delivery; Product Management operates at the ART level, focused on prioritizing and delivering Features that stem from those approved Epics.

**Does every Epic need a dedicated Epic Owner?**
Yes, within a properly functioning SAFe portfolio process — each Epic moving through the portfolio Kanban should have clear ownership to drive its definition and refinement, though the same person might serve as Epic Owner for multiple Epics.

**What happens to the Epic Owner's involvement once an Epic reaches implementation?**
Their primary defining and validating work is largely complete once an Epic reaches implementation, though they often remain available for ongoing coordination and questions as Solution Management and Product Management take on primary delivery responsibility.
