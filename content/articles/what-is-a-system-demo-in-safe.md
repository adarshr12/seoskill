# What Is a System Demo in SAFe?

A **System Demo** is a SAFe event, typically held at the end of each sprint, where all teams on an Agile Release Train come together to demonstrate their integrated work as a single, working system — distinct from an individual team's sprint review, which shows only that team's specific work in isolation.

## Quick facts

- The System Demo happens at the ART level, integrating work across all teams, unlike a team-level sprint review.
- It's typically held every sprint, giving stakeholders regular visibility into the ART's cumulative, integrated progress.
- This connects to [What Are the SAFe Ceremonies/Events](/safe/what-are-the-safe-ceremoniesevents-full-list), where the System Demo is one of several ART-level events.

## System Demo vs sprint review

| | Sprint Review | System Demo |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | One team's specific work | Integrated work across all ART teams |
| Frequency | Every sprint, per team | Every sprint, at the ART level |
| Purpose | Show and gather feedback on this team's specific increment | Show how all teams' work functions together as one system |
| Audience | Team's immediate stakeholders | Broader ART stakeholders and leadership |

## Why the System Demo matters

Individual team sprint reviews show valuable, but isolated, progress — they don't reveal whether the pieces built by different teams actually integrate and work together as a coherent whole. The System Demo specifically addresses this gap, giving the ART and its stakeholders regular, tangible visibility into whether the overall Program Increment objectives are genuinely being achieved as an integrated system, not just as separate, disconnected team-level increments.

## How a System Demo typically works

Representatives from across the ART's teams come together to present a working demonstration of the system, showing how each team's recent work contributes to and integrates with the others. This isn't simply a sequence of separate team demos — the goal is showing genuine end-to-end functionality, revealing whether the teams' individually completed work actually produces a coherent, working outcome when combined.

## A worked example

An ART building a connected vehicle platform holds a System Demo at the end of each sprint. Rather than each team (in-vehicle software, mobile app, backend infrastructure) demonstrating their work separately and in isolation, the System Demo shows an end-to-end scenario: a vehicle diagnostic alert generated by the in-vehicle software team's recent work, transmitted through the backend infrastructure team's recent work, and displayed to a user through the mobile app team's recent work — demonstrating that these three teams' separately-built pieces genuinely function together as an integrated system, not just as disconnected individual deliverables.

## Common mistakes with the System Demo

- **Treating it as a sequence of separate team demos** rather than a genuine, integrated demonstration of the system working end-to-end.
- **Skipping or deprioritizing the System Demo when integration challenges make it difficult**, missing exactly the visibility this event is meant to provide into real integration problems.
- **Not inviting the right stakeholders**, missing the broader visibility and feedback the event is designed to generate.
- **Only demonstrating polished, working scenarios** while hiding genuine integration issues, undermining the transparency this event is meant to provide.

## FAQ

**How is the System Demo different from Inspect & Adapt?**
The System Demo happens every sprint and focuses on showing integrated system functionality; [Inspect & Adapt](/safe/what-is-an-inspect-and-adapt-event-in-safe) happens once per Program Increment and combines a broader review with a structured, ART-wide retrospective.

**Who typically attends a System Demo?**
Representatives from all teams on the ART, along with broader stakeholders, business owners, and sometimes customers interested in the ART's overall progress.

**What if the teams' work doesn't yet integrate well by the System Demo?**
This is exactly the kind of issue the System Demo is meant to surface early — revealing integration problems during a regular System Demo is far better than discovering them only much later, closer to a planned release.

**Is a System Demo required every single sprint?**
Most SAFe implementations hold it every sprint to maintain regular, ongoing visibility into integrated progress, though the specific cadence can be adapted to an organization's particular context and needs.
