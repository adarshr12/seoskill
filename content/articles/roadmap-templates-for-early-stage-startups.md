# Roadmap Templates for Early-Stage Startups

Early-stage startups need a simpler roadmap than larger companies — one that can change quickly without a heavy planning process behind it. Below are two ready-to-use templates: a **Now-Next-Later** template for teams that want maximum flexibility, and a **lightweight quarterly** template for teams that need slightly more structure for investors or a growing team.

## Quick facts

- Early-stage roadmaps should prioritize flexibility over detail — a startup's priorities can shift dramatically within weeks.
- Both templates below are usable directly, without any special tool — a spreadsheet or a simple doc works fine at this stage.
- See the full format explanation in [Now-Next-Later Roadmap Format Explained](/product-roadmap/now-next-later-roadmap-format-explained).

## Template 1: Now-Next-Later (most flexible)

```
NOW (this month, actively being built)
- [Item] — why it matters right now
- [Item] — why it matters right now

NEXT (likely in the next 1-2 months, not yet started)
- [Item] — rough reasoning
- [Item] — rough reasoning

LATER (directionally planned, not committed)
- [Item] — the problem it would solve
- [Item] — the problem it would solve
```

This format avoids committing to specific dates, which is honest for a startup where priorities genuinely change fast — and it's easy to update in five minutes whenever priorities shift.

## Template 2: Lightweight quarterly (more structure)

```
QUARTER: [Q_ 20__]
GOAL: [The single main outcome this quarter is meant to achieve]

TOP 3 PRIORITIES
1. [Item] — connects to goal because: ___
2. [Item] — connects to goal because: ___
3. [Item] — connects to goal because: ___

ALSO IN PROGRESS (smaller, lower-priority work)
- [Item]
- [Item]

EXPLICITLY NOT THIS QUARTER
- [Item] — reason deferred
- [Item] — reason deferred
```

The "explicitly not this quarter" section is deliberately included — naming what's being deferred, and why, is often more useful for a small team's alignment than the list of what's planned.

## Which template fits your stage

| | Now-Next-Later | Lightweight Quarterly |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Pre-product-market-fit, changing weekly | Post-PMF, raising funds, or growing past ~10 people |
| Commitment level | Low — explicitly non-committal | Moderate — states a quarterly goal |
| Update frequency | As often as priorities shift | Reviewed each quarter, referenced in between |
| Audience | Internal team, early investors | Team, board, more structured stakeholders |

## How to adapt these templates as the startup grows

Start with Now-Next-Later while the product and priorities are genuinely uncertain — forcing quarterly commitments too early creates a roadmap that's technically followed but doesn't reflect reality. Move toward the lightweight quarterly template once the company has enough product-market signal that quarterly goals are meaningful, typically once there's a repeatable sense of what's working. Avoid adopting a heavier enterprise-style roadmap format before it's actually needed — added structure has a real cost in time and flexibility that most early-stage teams can't afford.

## Common mistakes with early-stage roadmap templates

- **Copying an enterprise-style roadmap template too early**, adding structure and commitment the startup doesn't have the stability to support yet.
- **Never updating the roadmap once created**, letting it silently go stale within weeks in a fast-moving early-stage environment.
- **Making the roadmap overly detailed**, spending time on granular planning that's likely to change before it matters.
- **Not stating the reasoning behind priorities**, even in a simple template — reasoning is what makes a roadmap useful to a small, fast-moving team, not just a list.

## FAQ

**Do early-stage startups need a formal roadmap tool?**
No — a simple document or spreadsheet using either template above is entirely sufficient; dedicated roadmap tools become more valuable once the team and stakeholder list grow larger.

**How often should an early-stage startup update its roadmap?**
The Now-Next-Later format should be revisited whenever priorities genuinely shift, often every few weeks; the quarterly format is reviewed each quarter but can still be adjusted mid-quarter if needed.

**Should investors see the internal roadmap directly?**
Often a simplified version works better — investors typically care most about the top priorities and the reasoning behind them, not the full granular internal detail.

**When should a startup move to a more detailed roadmap format?**
Generally once there's enough product-market signal and team size that quarterly commitments are meaningful and coordination across more people requires more structure than a simple Now-Next-Later list provides.
