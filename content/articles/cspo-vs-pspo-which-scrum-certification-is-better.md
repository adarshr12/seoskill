# CSPO vs PSPO: Which Scrum Certification Is Better?

**Verdict:** **PSPO** (Professional Scrum Product Owner, from Scrum.org) is generally better if you want a rigorous, exam-based credential that demonstrates tested knowledge at a lower cost, with no expiration. **CSPO** (Certified Scrum Product Owner, from Scrum Alliance) is generally better if you want structured, cohort-based training with direct instructor interaction and a strong professional network — but it costs more and requires renewal. Neither is universally "better"; the right choice depends on what you value more: rigorous testing or structured training and community.

## Quick facts

- CSPO is earned through course attendance (2 days), no exam; PSPO is earned by passing an exam after self-study or optional training.
- CSPO requires renewal every 2 years with a fee; PSPO certification doesn't expire.
- PSPO is generally cheaper overall, since it doesn't require an in-person or live course, though many candidates still take paid PSPO training.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | CSPO (Scrum Alliance) | PSPO (Scrum.org) |
|---|---|---|
| How it's earned | 2-day course attendance, no exam | Self-study or training, then a proctored exam |
| Typical cost | $400-1,200 (course fee) | $200-500 (exam fee, training optional and separate) |
| Expiration | Renews every 2 years, with a fee | Does not expire |
| Rigor | Tests attendance and participation, not a knowledge exam | Tests knowledge directly through a scored exam |
| Best for | Those who value structured, instructor-led training and Scrum Alliance networking | Those who want a rigorous, lower-cost, non-expiring credential |

## Why the "which is better" question doesn't have one universal answer

Both certifications are widely recognized in the industry, and neither is clearly superior across every dimension — they optimize for different things. CSPO's course-based format guarantees structured exposure to the material and creates a cohort of peers, which some learners genuinely value, especially those newer to product ownership who benefit from live instruction and discussion. PSPO's exam-based format demonstrates actual tested knowledge more rigorously and never expires, appealing to candidates who prefer self-directed study and want to avoid recurring renewal costs.

## How to choose between them

Consider CSPO if you're new to Scrum and product ownership and would benefit from structured, live training with direct instructor access and networking opportunities, and if the higher cost and renewal requirement aren't significant concerns. Consider PSPO if you prefer self-directed learning, want a credential that demonstrates tested knowledge rather than just attendance, want to avoid recurring renewal costs, and are comfortable studying independently (using Scrum.org's free "Scrum Open" practice assessments) before the exam.

## A worked example

A business analyst transitioning into a Product Owner role, new to Scrum concepts, chooses CSPO specifically because the structured 2-day course, complete with live discussion and instructor Q&A, helps them build foundational understanding faster than self-study would. A software developer with a few years of informal Scrum experience, comfortable with self-directed learning, instead chooses PSPO — studying independently using free online resources and Scrum.org's practice assessments, then passing the exam at a lower total cost and without a recurring renewal requirement.

## Common mistakes when choosing between CSPO and PSPO

- **Assuming one certification is objectively more respected than the other** — both are widely recognized, and the "better" choice depends on individual learning preference and goals, not a universal ranking.
- **Not factoring in the renewal cost of CSPO** when comparing total cost of ownership over several years.
- **Choosing PSPO without adequate self-study preparation**, since it's a scored exam that can be failed, unlike CSPO's attendance-based format.
- **Treating either certification as a substitute for real hands-on Product Owner experience**, when both are best viewed as complements to practical work, not replacements for it.

## FAQ

**Which certification is more widely recognized by employers?**
Both are widely recognized; regional and industry preferences vary, so checking job postings in your specific target market and location can help indicate which is more commonly requested.

**Can I get both CSPO and PSPO?**
Yes, some professionals pursue both over time, though for most people, one is sufficient to demonstrate Product Owner knowledge — pursuing both mainly makes sense if you specifically want exposure to both Scrum Alliance and Scrum.org's communities and resources.

**Is PSPO harder to pass than CSPO to obtain?**
In a sense, yes — PSPO requires passing a scored exam that tests genuine knowledge, while CSPO is earned through course completion without a separate exam, making PSPO's process inherently more rigorous as a knowledge test.

**Does either certification guarantee a Product Owner job?**
No — neither certification alone guarantees employment; both are best understood as supporting evidence of foundational knowledge alongside real experience, portfolio work, and interview performance.
