# How to Get Promoted From PM to Senior PM

**TL;DR**
- Demonstrate senior-level scope and judgment before the title change, not after — promotions typically follow evidence, not just tenure.
- Understand your specific company's actual promotion criteria, since these vary and aren't always obvious from a generic job description.
- Make your impact visible to the people who make the promotion decision, not just to your immediate team.

Getting promoted from PM to senior PM generally requires demonstrating the scope, judgment, and impact expected at the senior level *before* the title officially changes — most organizations promote based on evidence of already-operating-at-the-next-level, not primarily on tenure or a request alone.

## Quick facts

- Most promotions follow demonstrated evidence of senior-level work, not simply time in role.
- Company-specific promotion criteria often differ meaningfully from generic industry expectations — understanding your specific company's actual bar matters.
- This builds on [What Separates a Good PM From a Great PM](/pm-career/what-separates-a-good-pm-from-a-great-pm), covering the underlying judgment and scope differences this promotion typically reflects.

## How to get promoted from PM to senior PM, step by step

1. **Understand your specific company's actual promotion criteria.** Ask your manager directly what specifically differentiates a senior PM at your company — generic industry descriptions often don't match your specific organization's actual bar, and understanding the real, specific criteria is the necessary foundation for the rest of this process.
2. **Identify the specific gaps between your current work and the senior-level expectations.** Common gaps include broader scope of ownership, more ambiguous problem-solving, and more significant cross-functional influence — be honest about where you're currently falling short of these expectations.
3. **Proactively seek opportunities to demonstrate senior-level scope**, even before formally holding the title. Volunteering for a more ambiguous, higher-stakes project, or taking on broader cross-functional coordination, builds real evidence of already operating at the next level.
4. **Make your impact visible, not just felt by your immediate team.** Ensure the people who actually make promotion decisions are aware of your contributions — this might mean more visible communication, presenting your work more broadly, or your manager actively advocating on your behalf.
5. **Seek regular, specific feedback on your promotion readiness**, not just general performance feedback. Ask directly: "what specifically would need to be true for me to be ready for senior PM?" and treat the answer as a concrete, actionable checklist.
6. **Build a strong case with concrete evidence** when the promotion conversation happens — specific examples of senior-level scope, judgment, and impact you've already demonstrated, not just a general assertion that you're ready.
7. **Be patient but not passive.** Promotion timelines vary and aren't always purely merit-based on a predictable schedule, but consistently demonstrating readiness while also having direct conversations about your trajectory positions you better than either impatience or passive waiting alone.

## Why demonstrating before promotion matters more than requesting after tenure

Most organizations promote based on evidence that someone is already operating at the next level, not primarily on the basis of time served in the current role. This means the most effective path to promotion is proactively taking on the scope and responsibility associated with the next level *before* the title officially changes — waiting passively for tenure alone to justify a promotion request generally produces slower results than actively building demonstrable evidence of senior-level work.

## Why understanding company-specific criteria matters

"Senior PM" means genuinely different things at different companies — some emphasize scope of ownership, others emphasize technical depth, others emphasize cross-functional leadership. Generic industry descriptions of what a senior PM does can mislead you about what your *specific* company's promotion committee or manager is actually looking for. A direct conversation with your manager about your organization's specific criteria is a more reliable guide than general industry assumptions.

## A worked example

A PM wanting promotion to senior PM asks their manager directly what specifically differentiates the two levels at their company, learning that the key gap is demonstrated ability to lead cross-functional initiatives spanning multiple teams, not just owning a single team's roadmap well (which they already do competently). Over the following two quarters, they proactively volunteer to lead a cross-functional initiative involving three teams, deliberately building visible evidence of this specific gap being closed — communicating progress broadly, not just within their immediate team, and specifically highlighting the cross-functional coordination challenges they navigated. When the promotion conversation happens, they have concrete, specific evidence directly addressing the criteria their manager identified, rather than a general assertion of readiness.

## Common mistakes when pursuing this promotion

- **Waiting passively for tenure alone to justify promotion**, rather than proactively building demonstrable evidence of senior-level work.
- **Assuming generic industry-standard senior PM criteria apply**, without confirming your specific company's actual expectations.
- **Doing strong work that's only visible to your immediate team**, missing the broader visibility needed for promotion decision-makers to actually be aware of your impact.
- **Requesting promotion without concrete, specific evidence**, relying on a general sense of "I'm ready" rather than a compelling, specific case.

## FAQ

**How long does it typically take to get promoted from PM to senior PM?**
This varies enormously by company, individual performance, and organizational promotion cycles — there's no universal timeline, which is part of why demonstrating readiness proactively matters more than expecting a fixed schedule.

**Should I ask my manager directly about promotion, or wait to be offered one?**
Asking directly, and specifically requesting concrete criteria for what's needed, is generally more effective than passively waiting — most managers respond well to a genuine, proactive conversation about career growth.

**What if my manager's feedback about promotion readiness feels vague?**
Push for specificity — ask for concrete examples of what senior-level work looks like at your company, or reference specific recent situations and ask directly whether your handling of them reflected senior-level judgment.

**Is it common to switch companies to get promoted to senior PM faster?**
This does happen and can sometimes accelerate title progression, though it carries its own trade-offs (starting fresh at a new company, losing existing relationship equity) — whether it's the right move depends on your specific situation and how blocked your growth genuinely is at your current company.
