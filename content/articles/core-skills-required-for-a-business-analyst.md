# Core Skills Required for a Business Analyst

A business analyst needs a mix of analytical, communication, and technical skills — the ability to gather and document requirements clearly, understand business processes deeply, and translate between business needs and technical solutions. No single skill matters most; strong business analysts are effective because they combine several of these skills well, not because they excel at just one.

## Quick facts

- Communication and requirements-gathering skills are consistently ranked as most critical, ahead of specific technical tools.
- Many effective business analysts come from varied backgrounds (business, technical, or domain-specific), since the role rewards a mix of skills over one narrow specialization.
- See related: [What Does a Product Manager Do Day to Day](/product-management/what-does-a-product-manager-do-day-to-day) for how BA and PM skill sets overlap and differ.

## The core skills, organized by category

| Category | Key skills |
|---|---|
| Requirements & documentation | Requirements gathering, writing clear BRDs and functional specs, acceptance criteria |
| Analytical | Process mapping, root-cause analysis, data analysis, gap analysis |
| Communication | Stakeholder interviews, facilitation, clear written and verbal communication |
| Technical | Basic data querying (SQL), familiarity with system architecture, wireframing tools |
| Domain | Understanding of the specific industry or business function being analyzed |

## Requirements gathering and documentation

The ability to elicit real requirements from stakeholders — not just what they say they want, but the underlying business need — is foundational. This includes running effective workshops and interviews, asking clarifying questions that surface hidden assumptions, and writing the results down clearly enough that both business and technical audiences can act on them. See [How to Run a Requirements Gathering Workshop](/business-analyst/how-to-run-a-requirements-gathering-workshop) for the practical process.

## Analytical thinking and process mapping

Business analysts regularly need to understand and document how a business process currently works, identify where it breaks down, and map out an improved version. This requires genuine analytical skill: breaking a complex process into clear steps, identifying root causes rather than surface symptoms, and spotting gaps between current and desired states. See [What Is Process Mapping in Business Analysis](/business-analyst/what-is-process-mapping-in-business-analysis) for more detail.

## Stakeholder communication and facilitation

A business analyst regularly sits between different groups — business stakeholders, technical teams, leadership — each with different priorities and vocabulary. Strong communication skills mean being able to translate between these groups accurately, facilitate productive meetings where genuine alignment happens, and manage disagreements constructively when stakeholders have conflicting requirements.

## Basic technical literacy

Business analysts don't need to code, but a working understanding of how systems and data work — basic SQL for querying data, familiarity with how software gets built, and comfort with tools like wireframing software — significantly improves how effectively a BA can gather accurate requirements and communicate with technical teams.

## Why no single skill is enough on its own

A business analyst who's an excellent writer but poor at stakeholder interviews will document the wrong requirements clearly. One who's analytically sharp but communicates poorly will struggle to get stakeholder buy-in on their findings. The role's real value comes from combining these skills — understanding a business problem deeply, gathering accurate information about it, and communicating both the problem and the solution clearly to the people who need to act on it.

## Common mistakes when developing business analyst skills

- **Over-focusing on technical tools** (like a specific software certification) while under-investing in communication and facilitation skills, which matter more day-to-day for most BA roles.
- **Treating requirements gathering as a one-time interview** rather than an iterative process involving follow-up questions and validation as understanding develops.
- **Skipping process mapping and analytical rigor**, jumping straight from a stakeholder's stated request to a solution without genuinely understanding the underlying problem.
- **Underestimating the importance of clear written documentation**, which is often what different teams actually reference weeks or months after a conversation happened.

## FAQ

**Do business analysts need to know how to code?**
Not typically — basic technical literacy (like SQL for data queries) is valuable, but coding is not a core requirement for most business analyst roles, unlike more technical roles such as systems analyst.

**What's the most important skill for a new business analyst to develop first?**
Requirements gathering and clear communication tend to have the most immediate impact, since nearly every other BA responsibility depends on accurately understanding and documenting a business need first.

**Is a specific certification necessary to become a business analyst?**
Not strictly necessary, though certifications like the IIBA's CBAP or CCBA can help formalize and demonstrate skills, particularly for BAs without a traditional business analysis background.

**How do business analyst skills differ from product manager skills?**
There's significant overlap, particularly in requirements and stakeholder communication, but business analysts typically go deeper into process analysis and detailed documentation, while product managers focus more on strategic prioritization and product vision.
