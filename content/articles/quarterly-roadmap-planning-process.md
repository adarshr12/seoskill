# The Quarterly Roadmap Planning Process, Step by Step

A quarterly roadmap planning process is the recurring set of steps a product team runs at the start of each quarter to decide what to build next — reviewing what happened last quarter, gathering input, prioritizing candidates, and publishing an updated roadmap. Running this process consistently, on a predictable cadence, is usually more valuable than any single framework used inside it.

## Quick facts

- Quarterly planning typically takes 1-2 weeks of preparation before the quarter starts, not a single meeting.
- The process should always start with a review of the previous quarter, not a blank slate.
- A structured framework like [RICE](/product-frameworks/rice-framework-explained-with-example) keeps prioritization consistent from quarter to quarter.
- Connects to [How to Align a Roadmap With Company OKRs](/product-roadmap/how-to-align-a-roadmap-with-company-okrs), since most quarterly planning cycles map to the OKR cycle.

## The quarterly roadmap planning process, step by step

1. **Review the previous quarter honestly.** What shipped, what didn't, and what was actually learned from what shipped — including things that didn't produce the expected result. This review grounds the new plan in reality instead of starting fresh each time.
2. **Gather input from all key sources.** Customer feedback, support ticket themes, sales and CS input, engineering's technical debt list, and the company's current OKRs. Collecting this before prioritization starts prevents a rushed, incomplete candidate list.
3. **Build a full candidate list of everything being considered.** This should be larger than what will actually make the roadmap — the point of the next step is choosing among genuine options, not rubber-stamping a pre-decided list.
4. **Score and prioritize candidates using a consistent framework.** RICE, MoSCoW, or another structured method, applied the same way each quarter, so decisions are comparable and defensible over time.
5. **Draft the roadmap and pressure-test it internally first.** Share the draft with engineering leads and key stakeholders before it's final, catching feasibility issues or major disagreements while the plan is still easy to adjust.
6. **Finalize and communicate the roadmap broadly.** Publish it in whatever format the organization uses (see [Now-Next-Later](/product-roadmap/now-next-later-roadmap-format-explained)), explaining the reasoning behind top priorities, not just listing them.
7. **Set a mid-quarter check-in.** Roadmaps shift as new information arrives — a scheduled check-in partway through the quarter catches drift early rather than only at the next full planning cycle.

## Why a consistent process matters more than a perfect framework

Teams often spend more energy debating which prioritization framework to use than actually running the process consistently. A merely decent framework applied the same way every quarter, with genuine input-gathering and honest review, produces a more trustworthy roadmap than a theoretically superior framework applied inconsistently or skipped under time pressure. Consistency is what lets stakeholders learn to trust and anticipate the roadmap process itself, not just the outcomes.

## A worked example

A mid-size SaaS company runs quarterly planning in the final two weeks of each quarter. Week one: the PM reviews the prior quarter's results, collects input from sales, support, and customer interviews, and compiles a candidate list of around 25 items. Week two: the PM scores all candidates with RICE, drafts a roadmap prioritizing the top six, reviews it with engineering leads for feasibility, and presents the finalized roadmap to leadership with the reasoning behind each major choice. The new quarter starts with a roadmap already validated and communicated, rather than being finalized reactively in the first week.

## Common mistakes in quarterly roadmap planning

- **Skipping the review of the previous quarter**, losing the chance to learn from what did or didn't work before repeating similar decisions.
- **Starting prioritization before finishing input-gathering**, leading to a roadmap built on an incomplete picture of real needs and constraints.
- **Finalizing the roadmap without a feasibility check from engineering**, risking a plan that looks good on paper but isn't actually realistic.
- **Never revisiting the roadmap mid-quarter**, letting it silently go stale as new information invalidates parts of the original plan.

## FAQ

**How long should quarterly roadmap planning actually take?**
Most teams spend one to two weeks total, spread across review, input-gathering, prioritization, and communication — trying to compress it into a single meeting usually produces a shallower, less defensible plan.

**Should the roadmap be rebuilt from scratch each quarter?**
No — most items carry over or evolve from the previous roadmap; the process is more about reassessing priorities and incorporating new information than starting over completely each time.

**Who should be involved in quarterly roadmap planning?**
The product manager typically leads it, but meaningful input from engineering, design, sales, customer success, and leadership is what makes the resulting roadmap realistic and well-supported.

**What if priorities change significantly mid-quarter?**
A scheduled mid-quarter check-in is exactly for this — significant new information should prompt a genuine reassessment rather than waiting rigidly for the next full quarterly cycle.
